# Thought Leadership Article Template

## Purpose
A bylined opinion piece authored in the founder's voice, positioning them as the definitive industry expert on a specific topic. Designed for syndication, LinkedIn articles, and SIGNAL editorial placement.

---

## Metadata Block
```
TEMPLATE: thought-leadership
DOC_TYPE: thought-leadership
AUTHOR: {founder_name}
COMPANY: {company_name}
INDUSTRY: {industry}
TOPIC: {topic}
WORD_TARGET: 800-1200 words
BYLINE: {founder_name}, {founder_title} at {company_name}
SYNDICATION: SIGNAL Editorial, LinkedIn Article, Industry Publications
```

---

## Structure

### Headline (Provocative, Counterintuitive, or Declarative)
Format: "[Conventional wisdom] is wrong. Here's what actually works." OR "[Number] things [industry] keeps getting wrong about [topic]" OR "Why [bold claim]"

**Example:**
> The Construction Industry's Obsession with Efficiency Is Making Projects Slower

---

### Opening Hook (50–100 words)
Start with a specific scene, a counterintuitive data point, or a direct challenge to the reader's assumptions. Do NOT start with "I" or with a company pitch.

**Template:**
> [Specific scenario or observation from the field]. For decades, [industry] has [conventional approach]. But [unexpected reality]. Here's what [X years of experience / specific data] actually shows.

---

### The Problem (150–200 words)
Name the widespread belief or practice that is causing harm. Be specific. Name the failure mode without naming competitors.

**Sections:**
- What the industry believes
- Why that belief persists (incentive structure, legacy processes, misinformation)
- The real-world cost of that belief (data, examples, anecdotes)

---

### The Contrarian Insight (200–250 words)
Introduce the founder's specific point of view — the insight that comes from operating inside the problem. This is NOT a product pitch. It is a genuine intellectual contribution.

**Template:**
> After [X years / specific experience], I've learned that the real problem isn't [X]. It's [Y]. Here's why that distinction matters...

**Sub-sections:**
- The reframe (how to think about the problem differently)
- The evidence (what the founder has seen, measured, or built that validates this view)
- The implication (what changes if you accept this reframe)

---

### What Actually Works (200–250 words)
Practical, specific, actionable guidance. Not a sales pitch — a genuine contribution that readers can act on regardless of whether they work with the author.

**Template:**
> Based on [specific context], here's what we've found works:

1. **[Principle 1]** — [1-2 sentence explanation with specificity]
2. **[Principle 2]** — [1-2 sentence explanation with specificity]
3. **[Principle 3]** — [1-2 sentence explanation with specificity]

---

### The Broader Stakes (100–150 words)
Zoom out. Why does this matter beyond the founder's company? What is at stake for the industry, patients/workers/communities, or the economy if the industry keeps getting this wrong?

---

### Call to Action / Closing (50–100 words)
End with an invitation to continue the conversation — not a sales pitch. The author should invite debate, questions, or dialogue.

**Template:**
> [Restate the core insight in one sentence]. If you're working on [related problem] and have seen something different, I'd genuinely like to hear it. [LinkedIn / email CTA]

---

## Tone & Style Guidelines

- **Voice**: First-person, authoritative but not arrogant. The author has conviction but invites dialogue.
- **Specificity**: Every claim should have a specific example, number, or observation attached.
- **Jargon**: Industry-appropriate but not excessive. Define any technical terms.
- **Length**: 800–1,200 words. No filler. No meandering.
- **Perspective**: Written from the operator's lived experience, not from a marketing brief.

---

## Editorial Checklist

- [ ] Headline is counterintuitive or provocative (not generic)
- [ ] No company pitch in body text (mention company once in byline only)
- [ ] At least one specific data point or measurable outcome
- [ ] At least one specific anecdote or scene from the field
- [ ] Conclusion invites dialogue rather than selling
- [ ] Word count 800–1,200
- [ ] Passes editorial voice check (sounds like a real operator, not a press release)

---

## Syndication Notes

**LinkedIn Article version**: Add 3-sentence intro contextualizing why author is writing this now.
**SIGNAL placement**: Add "About the Author" 2-sentence bio at bottom.
**Industry publication pitch**: Lead with publication-specific angle, keep core argument identical.
