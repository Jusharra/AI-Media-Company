# LinkedIn Post Package Template

## Purpose
A 3-post LinkedIn content package for the featured founder. Posts are written in the founder's authentic voice for maximum organic reach. Designed to be published across 3 weeks after the main feature drops.

---

## Metadata Block
```
TEMPLATE: linkedin-post
DOC_TYPE: linkedin-post
SUBJECT: {founder_name}
COMPANY: {company_name}
INDUSTRY: {industry}
HOOK: {founder_hook}
FEATURE_URL: {signal_feature_article_url}
PACKAGE_POSTS: 3
RECOMMENDED_CADENCE: Week 1 / Week 2 / Week 3
```

---

## POST 1 — The Origin Story Post
**Purpose**: Maximum reach. Personal, vulnerable, story-driven. Posted Week 1 to coincide with feature article drop.
**Goal**: Comments, shares, new followers
**Tone**: Personal, specific, honest

---

### Structure

**[LINE 1 — The Hook]** (single line, no period, designed to stop the scroll)
> Something most people don't know about how I ended up building [COMPANY]:

OR:

> I spent [X] years in [industry] before I understood the problem that [COMPANY] solves.

OR:

> [Counterintuitive statement that challenges assumptions about founder's background or industry]

---

**[LINES 2–4 — The Scene]** (2-3 short sentences, specific moment in time)
> [Specific moment]. [What they saw / experienced]. [The realization].

---

**[LINES 5–12 — The Story]** (the background, the turning point, the build)
Structure:
- What their pre-founder life looked like (1-2 lines)
- The thing they saw that others missed (1-2 lines)
- The moment of decision (1 line)
- What they built and why (2-3 lines)
- The result / traction signal (1-2 lines)

---

**[CLOSING — The Invitation]** (1-2 lines)
> If you're building in [industry] or dealing with [problem], I'd love to hear what you're seeing. [OR: Link to full story below.]

---

**[CTA LINE]** (final line, optional link)
> My full story is covered in @SIGNAL this week → [link]

---

### Example (Healthcare)
```
Most people don't know I almost quit medicine at year 3 of residency.

The hospital I was in had a 22-minute average delay between OR turnover.
That doesn't sound like much.
But at 12 surgeries a day, that's 4+ hours of lost OR time — every single day.

I kept thinking: this is a scheduling problem, not a staffing problem.
Nobody wanted to hear that.

I spent two more years building the evidence inside one hospital.
Then left to build MedFlow.

In 18 months, we're in 14 hospitals.
Average turnover delay: 9 minutes.

I'm not telling you it was easy.
I'm telling you the problem was always solvable — it just needed someone who'd lived inside it.

If you're dealing with OR scheduling or hospital throughput, DM me.
I read every message.

Full story in SIGNAL this week → [link]
```

## POST 2 — The Insight Post
**Purpose**: Thought leadership. Share the core contrarian insight from the thought-leadership article. Posted Week 2.
**Goal**: Saves, shares to professional networks, engagement from industry
**Tone**: Confident, specific, slightly provocative

---

### Structure

**[LINE 1 — The Claim]** (direct, confident, slightly counterintuitive)
> [Industry] keeps focusing on [X].
> The real problem is [Y].

---

**[LINES 2–8 — The Argument]**
Structure:
- What the conventional approach is (1-2 lines)
- Why it doesn't work (1-2 lines)
- What they've learned from their specific vantage point (2-3 lines)
- The reframe (1-2 lines)

Use short paragraphs. Each paragraph = 1-2 sentences max.

---

**[LINES 9–12 — The Evidence]**
> Here's what we've seen after [X months / Y customers / Z projects]:
>
> [Specific data point 1]
> [Specific data point 2]
> [Specific observation or pattern]

---

**[CLOSING]**
> I'm curious: what are you seeing from your side? Drop a comment or DM me.

---

### Example (Construction)
```
Construction keeps optimizing for speed.
The real bottleneck is information.

Every GC I talk to has the same problem:
By the time a subcontractor delay surfaces, you've already lost a week.

We used to think the answer was faster communication.
Turns out it's earlier visibility.

The projects that run on time don't have better contractors.
They have better information earlier in the process.

After 200 sites on Construct AI:

— Projects with our risk flagging system: avg 2.1-day delay
— Projects without: avg 9.8-day delay
— Same subcontractors. Different visibility.

The information was always there.
It just wasn't organized to be actionable.

What's your experience? Drop a comment — I read them all.
```

## POST 3 — The Lesson Post
**Purpose**: Practical value. Share 3-5 specific lessons from the founder's experience. Posted Week 3.
**Goal**: Saves, high-quality engagement, email signups
**Tone**: Generous, specific, practical

---

### Structure

**[LINE 1 — The Frame]**
> [X] lessons I learned building [COMPANY] in [time period]:
> (that [industry type] schools don't teach)

---

**[LESSONS BLOCK]** (3–5 lessons, numbered)
Format for each:
```
[NUMBER]. [Short, memorable lesson title]

[2-3 sentence explanation with specific example or outcome from their experience]
```

---

**[CLOSING]**
> The [industry] is harder than most outsiders think.
> It's also more winnable than most insiders believe.
>
> Building in [industry]? I'd love to connect.

---

### Example (Oil & Gas)
```
5 things I learned building energy software that nobody warned me about:

1. The procurement cycle is a feature, not a bug.

We spent our first year trying to speed up the sales process.
Then we realized: operators who move fast are operators who haven't done their diligence.
The 9-month sales cycle means you get serious customers.

2. Trust is the actual product.

Every tool we built, we thought was the value.
It wasn't. The value was our customers trusting us with operational data they'd never shared with a vendor before.
Earn that first. Everything else follows.

3. The problem isn't technology. It's change management.

Our first 10 deployments failed for the same reason:
The technology worked. The middle management layer wasn't bought in.
Now we spend 40% of implementation time on operator training and buy-in.

4. Your competitors are not who you think they are.

We thought we were competing against other software vendors.
We were competing against spreadsheets and tribal knowledge.
Never underestimate how deeply embedded the spreadsheet is.

5. Build for the field first, not the C-suite.

Every enterprise sale starts at the top.
Every implementation lives or dies with the people actually using it.
If it's not fast on a tablet with a glove on, it doesn't matter how good the dashboard looks.

Building in energy / oil & gas? Connect with me here.
I share what's working (and what isn't) every week.
```

## Post Formatting Notes

- **Line breaks**: Single-spaced lines, double space between paragraphs
- **No hashtags in body**: LinkedIn algorithm no longer boosts hashtag-heavy posts; add 2-3 to comments if desired
- **No bold/italic**: LinkedIn strips most formatting; write for plain text
- **Length**: Post 1: 250-350 words. Post 2: 150-250 words. Post 3: 250-400 words.
- **Posting time**: Tue/Wed/Thu, 7-9am local time
- **First comment**: Post the SIGNAL article link in the first comment, not the post body (increases organic reach)

---

- [ ] Post 1 approved by founder for tone/accuracy
- [ ] Post 2 reviewed for any claims that need softening
- [ ] Post 3 lessons verified as authentic to founder's experience
- [ ] Article URL added to Post 1 / first comment
- [ ] Scheduled in LinkedIn scheduling tool or Hootsuite/Buffer
- [ ] Founder briefed on responding to comments within 1 hour of posting
