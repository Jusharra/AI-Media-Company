# Twitter / X Thread Template

## Purpose
A 10–15 tweet thread written in the founder's voice. Designed for maximum reach, bookmarks, and profile follows. Each thread is built around one core insight, result, or story from the feature.

---

## Metadata Block
```
TEMPLATE: twitter-thread
DOC_TYPE: twitter-thread
SUBJECT: {founder_name}
COMPANY: {company_name}
INDUSTRY: {industry}
THREAD_HOOK: {core_hook}
FEATURE_URL: {signal_feature_article_url}
TWEET_COUNT: 10-15
```

---

## Thread Types

### Type A: The Story Thread
Best for: Compelling origin stories, turning points, dramatic pivots
Format: Narrative arc — problem → journey → outcome → lesson

### Type B: The Insight Thread
Best for: Contrarian views, industry analysis, counterintuitive findings
Format: Claim → evidence → implications → invitation

### Type C: The Lessons Thread
Best for: Practical knowledge, "what I wish I'd known", operator wisdom
Format: Numbered lessons with specific detail

---

## Thread Structure — Type A (Story)

---

**TWEET 1 — The Hook** (the entire thread has to earn from this)
Rules:
- 1-2 sentences max
- Specific, surprising, or emotionally resonant
- No "thread:" or "🧵" in tweet 1 — write a real hook
- Should work as a standalone tweet

Format options:
```
[Specific claim about outcome]:
[Setup that makes reader want to know more]

Thread:
```
OR:
```
[Counterintuitive statement about founder's background or industry]

Here's what I learned: (1/)
```

---

**TWEETS 2–4 — The Before**
- Where the founder was before (industry, role, what they saw)
- The problem they witnessed firsthand
- Why it bothered them when others accepted it

Format: Short sentences. One idea per tweet. Each tweet = 1 scene or 1 thought.

---

**TWEETS 5–7 — The Turn**
- The decision to build
- The early days / what they got wrong
- The first signal that it was working

---

**TWEETS 8–11 — The Build**
- How the product/company actually works (non-jargon)
- Specific outcome/traction/result
- The hardest thing they had to learn

---

**TWEETS 12–13 — The Lesson**
- The core insight that applies beyond their specific company
- What the industry needs to understand

---

**TWEET 14 — The Invitation**
```
If you're building in [industry] or dealing with [problem], follow me.
I share what's working (and what isn't) every week.
```

---

**TWEET 15 — The CTA**
```
Full story on @SIGNAL_media: [link]

If this was useful, RT tweet 1 to share it with someone building in [industry].
```

---

## Thread Structure — Type C (Lessons)

**TWEET 1 — The Frame**
```
I spent [X] years in [industry] before founding [COMPANY].

Here are [N] things I wish I'd known before I started:
```

---

**TWEETS 2–[N+1] — The Lessons**
One lesson per tweet. Format:
```
[Number]. [Memorable 1-sentence lesson]

[2-3 sentences of specific explanation]
[One concrete example from their experience]
```

Each lesson should:
- Have a specific number/detail attached if possible
- Challenge a common assumption
- Be genuinely useful to someone in the industry

---

**FINAL TWEET — The Wrap + CTA**
```
These took me [X] years to learn.
Hope they save you some time.

Follow me for more from inside [industry].
Full feature on SIGNAL: [link]
```

## Example Thread — Type A (Healthcare)

```
Tweet 1:
I walked out of a 14-hour OR shift knowing I was going to quit medicine.

Six months later I raised $2M and started a company.

Here's what happened in between:

Tweet 2:
I was a cardiothoracic surgeon for 9 years.
I loved the work.
I hated watching 20% of OR capacity evaporate every week because of scheduling chaos.

Tweet 3:
The hospital wasn't stupid.
It was working exactly as designed.
The problem: it was designed in 1987, before the data to fix this existed.

Tweet 4:
I started tracking OR delays obsessively.
On my own time. On paper. For 8 months.
The pattern was so obvious it made me angry.

Tweet 5:
I brought it to administration.
They said: "We've tried to fix this before."
I said: "You tried before the data was real-time."

Tweet 6:
They gave me a 90-day pilot.
One OR suite. My spreadsheet model.
Result: 34% reduction in turnover time.

Tweet 7:
That pilot became the proof.
I raised a seed round from two surgeons who'd seen the same problem.
MedFlow was born.

Tweet 8:
How it works (without the jargon):
→ Real-time OR status tracking
→ ML-predicted turnover windows
→ Automated staff coordination
→ No new hardware. Works on phones already in the building.

Tweet 9:
We're now in 14 hospitals.
Average OR turnover delay: dropped from 21 min → 9 min.
That's 2-3 additional surgeries per OR per day.
At average OR revenue? You do the math.

Tweet 10:
The hardest part wasn't the tech.
It was convincing a hospital that a surgeon could build software.
Turns out being the customer is the best qualification.

Tweet 11:
What I learned that I wish I'd known:

The incumbent solution isn't your competitor.
Status quo is your competitor.
Everything else is a distraction.

Tweet 12:
Healthcare is resistant to change not because the people are bad.
It's because the incentive structures punish early adoption and reward waiting.

You have to make the risk of NOT changing feel bigger than the risk of changing.

Tweet 13:
If you're a clinician thinking about building:
The thing that makes you doubt yourself (no business background, no CS degree) is also the thing that will make you dangerous.
Don't let anyone tell you the domain expertise isn't the hard part.

Tweet 14:
I share what I'm learning building at the intersection of clinical ops and AI.
Follow me if that's relevant to you.

Tweet 15:
SIGNAL did a full feature on MedFlow and the OR scheduling problem.
It's the most honest account I've given of how this actually happened.

Link: [url]

RT tweet 1 if you know a clinician or healthcare builder who should see this.
```

## Formatting Rules

- **Character limit**: 280 chars per tweet (aim for 200 to allow quote-tweets)
- **Line breaks**: Use line breaks to create visual breathing room — Twitter renders them
- **Emoji**: Use sparingly. → arrows are acceptable. 🧵 optional in thread numbering only
- **Numbers**: Use specific numbers when you have them. "2-3 additional surgeries" beats "more surgeries"
- **Threads**: Number each tweet as (2/) (3/) etc. or let the thread reply chain speak for itself
- **Links**: Only in final tweet. Links reduce reach on tweet 1.
